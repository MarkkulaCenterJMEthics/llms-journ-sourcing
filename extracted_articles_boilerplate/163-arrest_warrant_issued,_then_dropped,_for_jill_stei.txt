Title: Arrest warrant issued, then dropped, for Jill Stein after court no-show
Author: Marni Rose McFall
Date: 2026-08-18
URL: https://www.newsweek.com/arrest-warrant-issued-for-jill-stein-after-court-no-show-12335181
==================================================

An arrest warrant was issued, and then subsequently dropped, for the Green Party's former presidential candidate Dr. Jill Stein following a failed court appearance related to a 2024 assault charge.
The case had a hearing on Monday at 9 a.m. Neither Stein nor her attorney attended, though prosecutors were in attendance.
St. Louis Judge Karma Johnson said of Stein’s absence, “There will be a failure to appear warrant for Ms. Stein,” per St. Louis Magazine.
The warrant was later dropped after Stein's attorney, Joseph Welch, appeared in court in the afternoon and Judge Johnson granted his request to have the warrant quashed.
Stein said in a statement shared with Newsweek over email that "I hope the media pays more attention to the carnage in Gaza and the West Bank than to a warrant issued over a schedule mix up and recalled by the judge before I even had a chance to comment."
Welch said in a statement shared with Newsweek over email that "It was me, I screwed up the calendar and was late. The judge fixed it."
The charges stem from a protest at Washington University in April of 2024, over the war in Gaza. Over 100 arrests were made when police broke up the protest, but only two charges followed–Stein and one other individual.
Stein was charged in March 2025 and faced misdemeanors for assault and trespass.
Violation of Campus Activities
According to a report from the outlet Spectrum News from March of 2025, when charges were filed, the protest began in the area of Forest Park and then moved to the Washington University campus.
Protestors were told to leave by the associate vice chancellor, Dean Rob Wild, who cited a violation of campus activities. A final warning was issued before police officers approached.
Stein Allegedly Kicked Police Officer
A police officer, named Conroy, approached Stein on a bicycle, which she allegedly grabbed.
A police officer named Moore then intervened, and the bike allegedly injured his forearm. Stein allegedly kicked Moore, and he was later found to have a strained forearm and injuries to his leg and groin.
A statement from Washington University about the protest issued at the time said that a “large group” had entered the campus “with the intention of causing a significant disruption to the university.”
The statement noted that students and employees of the university were present “as well as many individuals not affiliated with the university.”
What Stein Has Said About The Incident
In an interview with the local news station KSDK after the incident, Stein denied the allegations and said the incident was "Kind of scary to tell you the truth."
"I was charged with assaulting a police officer. Can you believe it?" she said.
"I think it's a really bad look for the university," Stein said. She said the incident was "shameful," and said that it was a freedom of speech issue.
Of the altercation, Stein said that she was "assaulted by a bicycle," and "thrown onto her head."
Not a Freedom of Speech Issue
“We are firmly committed to free expression and allow ample opportunity for voices to be heard on our campus. However, we expect everyone to respect our policies and we will take swift action to enforce them to their fullest extent,” the university statement read.
When the charges were first filed in 2025, the St. Louis Prosecuting Attorney Melissa Price Smith said, “I want to make it clear that these misdemeanor criminal charges are not a statement about the freedoms of speech or assembly, which we honor and uphold as part of our Constitution.”
“They are not a statement about the subject of the protest, which was the situation in Gaza,” Smith added.
Stein's Role in Third-party Politics
Stein is an environmental activist and politician who was the Green Party’s nominee for president in 2012, 2016 and 2024.
She is a prominent figure in third-party politics and has regularly argued that the Democratic and Republican parties do not adequately address issues including climate change.
In the 2024 election, it was suggested that Stein and the Green party siphoned votes which likely would have gone to Democratic nominee Kamala Harris in key swing states, including Michigan and Wisconsin. Both of those states are home to large Muslim populations, many of whom supported Stein over Harris, in opposition to the Biden administration’s stance on Israel's war in Gaza.
Stein remains politically active and regularly posts on social media. On August 16, she wrote, “Don't surrender to the sheepdogs trying to stuff you back into the Democratic Party. America is sick of oligarchy, endless war, genocide & the duopoly enabling it all. Demand ranked choice voting, proportional rep, publicly funded elections, & parties not funded by billionaires!”
She has not publicly addressed the arrest warrant as of reporting on Tuesday morning.
Judge Johnson has continued the case until September 14, when Stein is set to appear in court.
What Happens After a Failure to Appear?
A failure to appear can mean that law enforcement can take the individual into custody if they are to encounter them.
However, the warrant can also be resolved by voluntarily appearing before the court or through action by an attorney.
In Missouri misdemeanor cases, judges often give defendants an opportunity to explain their absence, particularly if there was a scheduling conflict, communication issue or other mitigating circumstance. The court could choose to recall the warrant and set a new hearing date, but prosecutors may also ask that additional conditions be imposed to ensure future appearances.
Failure to appear does not amount to a conviction on the underlying charges.
