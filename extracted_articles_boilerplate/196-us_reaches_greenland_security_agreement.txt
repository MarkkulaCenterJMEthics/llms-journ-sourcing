POLITICS NATIONAL SECURITY
U.S. Reaches Greenland Security Agreement
By Alexander Ward
Updated Sept. 18, 2026 7:41 pm ET
Nuuk, Greenland, in June. JULIETTE PAVY/BLOOMBERG NEWS
WASHINGTON—President Trump said the U.S. and Denmark reached an
agreement that allows for more American forces in Greenland, though it
stops short of the self-governing island’s takeover that he envisioned since
the start of his second term.
“The United States will FOREVER have the complete ability to do what is
necessary in Greenland,” Trump wrote Friday on social media. “We will
immediately begin the process of developing a large Military presence in the
appropriate part of Greenland, of which there are many. We will work with
the people of Greenland in its development and construction.”
The governments of Greenland and Denmark, which has sovereignty over
the island, confirmed they would sign the agreement during the United

Nations General Assembly next week.
“It is gratifying that we are about to enter into an agreement that ensures
and strengthens the security of Greenland, the Kingdom of Denmark, the
United States and the Western alliance,” Danish Prime Minister Mette
Frederiksen said in a statement.
Jens-Frederik Nielsen, Greenland’s leader, said “the agreement recognizes
Greenland’s interests and our place in international cooperation. This is to
the benefit of us all.”
The U.S. will have a permanent military presence on Greenland, even if it
becomes an independent nation, allowing for an increase in the number of
bases there, a State Department official said. The agreement also bans
countries who aren’t part of the North Atlantic Treaty Organization—
notably China and Russia—from establishing their own base or making
investments on the island that threaten U.S. security, the official said.
“Greenland will forever be part of the strategic defense area of North
America and exclude any adversary from it and the surrounding area,”
Secretary of State Marco Rubio said.
The deal Trump describes falls short of his stated desire for Greenland to
become sovereign U.S. territory. It instead tracks more closely with
Denmark’s counteroffer to enhance the U.S. military presence on the island
and give the U.S. preferential business deals. It reinforces concessions to the
U.S. similar to what already existed in a 2004 treaty, which gave the military
broad freedom to operate on the ice-covered island.
Greenland, as part of Denmark, is already protected NATO territory. Allies
were incensed when Trump raised the possibility of a military operation
against a fellow NATO member to seize the territory.
Denmark had already blocked NATO adversaries from making sensitive
business investments in Greenland, especially for rare earths, and was
unlikely to allow countries like China and Russia to establish a military base

on allied territory, analysts said. Long before Trump expressed a desire to
obtain the island, Denmark blocked Chinese investments there.
Successive U.S.-Danish agreements, dating back to the early months of Nazi
Germany’s occupation of Denmark, have given the American military broad
rights to station troops, build bases, operate ports or fly warplanes in
Greenland.
The U.S. has about 150 troops in Greenland stationed at Pituffik Space Base
and already has broad operational freedom, U.S. and Danish officials have
said. The U.S. and Danish jet fighters are currently participating in a military
exercise in Greenland.
Vice President JD Vance visited the facility last year as the White House
ramped up its campaign to control the island and its roughly 56,000
inhabitants. Polling shows Greenlanders consistently resisting any efforts to
officially join the U.S.
