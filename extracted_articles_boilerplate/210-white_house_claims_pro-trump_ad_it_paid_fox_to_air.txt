The White House on Friday defended its decision to use taxpayer funds to
buy airtime on Fox News for a White House-produced, pro-Trump ad that
some Democrats said could be illegal government propaganda aimed at
influencing voters in the upcoming midterm elections.
In a lengthy statement sent to news outlets, the White House press office
insisted that versions of the ad, which feature audio of Trump urging voters
to help him “defeat communism” and a whirlwind video review of his

second term, were simply routine “public service announcements” like those
produced by previous administrations.
However, while the Trump administration ad features the president in
almost every frame, and includes words of praise for him offered by a
prominent supporter during the 2024 campaign, none of the advertising
from three prior administrations the White House called similar featured any
partisan messaging or showed images of Trump’s predecessors.
By contrast, the 30-second version of the pro-Trump ad broadcast on Fox
News this week uses audio of Trump telling supporters in Las Vegas last
month: “Together we will defeat communism, socialism and Marxism in
America,” and a rapid-fire montage of images of Trump beneath the onscreen
text: “America will never be a communist country,” as a chorus sings the
words “love me”, taken from a song of that name by JMSN. In the original
speech, Trump explicitly identified the enemy as “Dumocrat communists in
Congress”.
The last 10 seconds of the ad include text at the bottom the screen saying:
“Paid for by the US government.”
The ad aired on Fox News this week as Trump hosted the leader of the
world’s largest communist nation, China, for a lavish state visit.
In its new statement, the White House insisted that versions of the ad
celebrating Trump, which were also posted on official government accounts
on TikTok and YouTube before airing on Fox, “are very clearly not campaign
ads; President Trump is not on the ballot and there is no call to action.
Instead, the announcements are a reminder for Americans to love their
country and know why it’s worth defending – at home, at the border, and
abroad.”

A 15-second version of an ad praising Donald Trump posted on the White House YouTube
channel lacks the political slogans included in a longer edit broadcast on Fox News this week.
In fact, the version of the ad broadcast on Fox clearly includes partisan
messaging that celebrates Trump’s presidency and features his rhetoric
comparing Democrats to communists in the run-up to congressional
elections in which he recently urged supporters to “pretend I’m on the
ballot” and vote for his party.
Unlike the initial versions of the ad posted on social media, the broadcast
version includes onscreen text boasting about what Trump calls his
accomplishments. “Largest tax cuts in history,” one slogan reads. “Reigniting
American manufacturing,” says another. “Defend law and order and police,”
comes next.
The broadcast version of the ad also closes with a tribute to Trump from
Dana White, the Ultimate Fighting Championship chief executive, who can
be heard saying: “He is the toughest, most resilient human being I have ever
met in my life.” White used that formulation to describe Trump multiple
times during the 2024 presidential election campaign, most notably to sing
Trump’s praises at the Republican national convention.
While the White House argues that the ad is an “unapologetically American”
public service announcement “aimed at the country – not a line on the
ballot”, the actual images used are not of the grandeur of the country or its
people, but just its current president.

In fact, slowing down the rapid-fire montage reveals that the flash-frame
imagery in the ad is exclusively of Trump commanding the stage in a bizarre
mix of moments during his second term, including: overseeing the US
military raid on Venezuela; hosting a state dinner for Saudi crown prince
Mohammed bin Salman; accepting the Fifa peace prize; pardoning a turkey;
announcing a new class of US navy ships to be named for himself; attending
a Kennedy Center performance; awarding the Fifa Club World Cup trophy;
showing Cristiano Ronaldo his presidential walk of fame; and addressing
McDonald’s owners.

The paid advertising by prior administrations which the Trump White House
compared its ad to on Friday were uniformly non-partisan public
information campaigns. A 2002 Super Bowl ad from the White House office
of national drug control policy, which connected drug use by young
Americans to funding terrorist attacks, made no mention of political parties

did not show or mention Barack Obama. A 2021 ad from the department of

health and human services to promote the Covid-19 vaccine similarly made
no mention of the president at the time, Joe Biden.
The White House defense of its ad comes after the representative Jamie
Raskin, a Maryland Democrat and constitutional scholar, suggested on social
media that the ad may violate federal laws including the Hatch Act.
“This may be felony criminal theft and conversion of government property
for political campaign purposes,” Raskin wrote. “It is a Hatch Act violation
for any government employees who worked on it or used government
resources to make it. It is blatantly unethical under numerous federal ethics
provisions proscribing the use of government resources for political
campaigns.”
AdImpact, a firm that tracks political ads, told the Wall Street Journal that
the pro-Trump ad aired on Fox News in New York City, Los Angeles, Miami
and Washington DC, as well as on Newsmax in Chicago.
The administration has spent at least $443,000 so far to air that ad, according
to the Associated Press.
AdImpact reported on Friday that the White House also paid to broadcast a
second ad featuring Trump, with excerpts from his 3 July address at Mount
Rushmore. The second ad, which aired on the rightwing network Newsmax
on Friday, includes images of the president’s supporters waving Trump flags
and ends with the president repeating a slogan he has used dozens of times
to describe his second administration: “This is only the beginning of the
golden age of America.”
JMSN, the Detroit-born artist who wrote and performs the song Love Me,
wrote on social media that it had been used “illegally”, because he was not
approached by the White House for permission. “I would never authorize my
music to be used for ANY political agenda or campaign,” the singer wrote. “I
am looking at lawyers handling this.”
