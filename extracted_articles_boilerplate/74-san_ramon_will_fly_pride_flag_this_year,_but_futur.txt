Title: San Ramon will fly Pride flag this year, but future is uncertain
Date: 2026-04-29
URL: https://www.ebar.com/story/165738/News/San%20Ramon%20will%20fly%20Pride%20flag%20this%20year%2C%20but%20future%20is%20uncertain
==================================================

While a flag celebrating the LGBTQ community will fly outside San Ramon City Hall this June for Pride Month, its future is uncertain. On Tuesday, members of the public addressed the city council ahead of it considering the fate of commemorative flags through an upcoming policy committee.
No action was taken at the April 28 meeting.
The San Ramon City Council voted 3-1 April 14 to fly the Progress Pride flag this year, with Councilmember Robert Jweinat casting the opposing vote after questioning the practice, according to a news release from the San Ramon Valley Diversity Coalition. That version of the Pride flag features the standard six colors of the rainbow flag and a chevron of black, brown, light blue, pink, and white stripes to represent Black, Brown, and trans people.
The East Bay council also voted to review its policy of raising commemorative flags after public opposition was voiced to raising the Pride flag. Two weeks later, community members gathered at the April 28 council meeting, which was livestreamed, and spoke during public comment.
Nineteen of the 24 speakers came to state their support for the raising of the Pride flag and to voice concern over the council’s decision to review the commemorative flag-raising policy. The other five addressed unrelated issues. Speakers were limited to two minutes each.
The resolution called into question is the Use of City Flag Poles and Flag Display Policy created in 2021. It authorizes the flying of commemorative flags at the request of a councilmember on a commemorative flag pole erected at Alcosta Boulevard and Bollinger Canyon outside City Hall. The Pride flag is to fly on it throughout June and be shown on the building’s marquee per the resolution submitted by City Councilmember Sridhar Verose.
Anuradha Gupta, the chapter president of PFLAG Danville-San Ramon Valley, wrote in an email, “We are grateful to the San Ramon mayor and councilmembers who voted to raise the Pride flag this year and distressed that this long-standing civic tradition will be reviewed at a time when there are unprecedented attacks on the LGBTQ+ community.”
Gupta was referring to policies by President Donald Trump and his administration that, among other things, have stripped rights from trans people.
Some speakers were concerned parents of LGBTQ youth and many described themselves as long-term San Ramon residents. Gupta wrote in an email that members from the local groups PFLAG Danville-San Ramon Valley and San Ramon Valley Diversity Coalition were in attendance, as well as members from Danville Congregational Church and Indivisible Tri-Valley.
“I was really happy to hear that this year, too, the Progressive flag will fly,” said Shailaja Dixit, a longtime resident of San Ramon, “but I was also deeply disturbed to hear that there was a single, lone vote cast against it, and that the commemorative flag policy is now going under review. Under current times, this feels very threatening.”
During the April 14 City Council meeting, the routine procedure of passing the resolution for raising the Pride flag was called into question after former mayor Greg Carr, Citizens Against Marketplace Apartment Development leader Susie Ferris-Inderkum, and a citizen calling themself “Make San Ramon Great Again” spoke in opposition to the commemorative flag resolution during public comment.
Jweinat made the motion to pull the commemorative flag-raising resolution from the consent calendar to review the policy, echoing former mayor Carr’s belief that only the U.S. flag, California state flag, and San Ramon city flag should be flown.
For several of the speakers, the Pride flag-raising represents safety, inclusion, and belonging in San Ramon for either themselves or their children. One speaker, a student at Gale Ranch Middle School, said that the Pride flag shows that San Ramon is diverse and respects people's choices.
Allison Gardner, a teacher in San Ramon, said, “We deserve much more than to have our fight with the city to fly a small piece of fabric one month a year to tell us maybe, just maybe, we do actually belong here in San Ramon.”
This is one of many debates over Pride flag displays happening across the country, and is taking place six years after the Bay Area Reporter extensively reported on the efforts of LGBTQ community members and allies to see the rainbow flag and more diverse versions of it flown outside city halls across the Bay Area during the height of the 2020 COVID epidemic when Pride parades and public gatherings were canceled.
Taking the opposite tact from San Ramon, the Petaluma City Schools Board of Education recently voted to display a Pride flag year-round instead of only during the month of June, KSRO radio reported.
The U.S. Department of the Interior recently announced its intention to return the Pride flag to the Stonewall National Monument in New York City, after its directive issued January 21 called for the removal of “non-agency” flags from National Parks. A nationwide uproar led to a court-enforceable settlement between the Trump administration and LGBTQ legal advocates calling for the flag to be raised again.
After widespread public backlash, Philz Coffee will keep Pride flags in its cafes, including its San Francisco locations such as the Castro LGBTQ neighborhood.
Per state law, the San Ramon councilmembers did not respond to any of the comments from the public, as they are unable to discuss items that are not on the meeting agenda. Their decision on the matter will be reserved for an upcoming policy review meeting, the exact date of which has yet to be set.
“Minority interest recognition allows a community to adapt to changing interests over time, and for an ability to remain relevant and vibrant,” said Bruce Hixon, one of the first speakers of the night. “While minority inclusion may seem imperfect, it should not be traded off for exclusion.”
