## Minor (v61)

1. **Body-only annotation scope.** *Added 2026-10-08; revised 2026-11-05: photo captions no longer count (`development-of-v59.md` item 69).* Reminder set for 2026-10-09: decide the exact prompt wording and where it goes.* Rule adopted 2026-10-08 (`development-of-v59.md` items 51 and 53). The current user prompt's step 1 only says "ignore the Subtitle"; it doesn't cover scrolling-graphic text panels, summary boxes, photo captions, graphics, or images of documents. The human-facing instruction below is final and in use. For the LLM prompt, replace the subtitle sentence in step 1 (and the "(Ignore the Subtitle text if it exists)" note in the opening line) with a short version, e.g.: *"Annotate only the body of the article: its paragraphs and any article-style text panels. Ignore the headline, subtitle, summary boxes ('Summary', 'Key points', etc.), photo captions, and any text from charts, maps or tables."* Since extraction now strips subtitles, summary boxes, captions and graphic text before the model sees the article (`article_body()` in `v10-extract-multiple-LLMs.py`), this line is a backstop for anything that slips through.

   Human-facing instruction (as adopted):

   > **What to annotate: the body of the article**
   >
   > Annotate sourced statements **only in the body of the article**. The body includes:
   > - every paragraph of the article's main text, from first to last
   > - **text panels in a scrolling graphic** (sometimes called "scrollytelling"), when they read as ordinary article paragraphs
   >
   > **Do not annotate:**
   > - the **headline** or the **subtitle** (the summary line just under the headline)
   > - **photo captions** (the text under a photo, and its credit line)
   > - **summary boxes**, such as "Summary," "Key points," "In summary," "Overview," or "Need to know"
   > - anything that belongs to a **chart, map, table, or other data graphic**: its title, description, labels, notes, or "Source:" line
   > - text that appears **only inside an image**, such as a screenshot of an email or document
   > - **bylines, datelines, author bios,** newsletter sign-ups, ads, "Read more" links, and other page navigation
   >
   > **One thing that is body text:** a sentence in the article that describes a graphic and says where its data comes from, such as *"The map below shows which ZIP codes have the most older homes, according to Census data."* Annotate it as usual.
   >
   > **Why:** Subtitles and summary boxes repeat what the body already says. Photo captions, graphics and images aren't part of the plain text the models receive. Annotating only the body means human and model annotations are measured against the same text.
   >
   > **If a statement seems important but you only find it in a subtitle, summary box, caption or graphic,** look for the same information in the body. If the body has it, annotate the body version. If it doesn't, leave it out.

3. **Source Descriptors: what to strip from a descriptor and what to keep** *[Folded into the consolidated v61 SD draft below, 2026-11-06.]* (Prompt Updates 21 and 30 merged, 2026-11-05; place rule decided the same day, `development-of-v59.md` item 70; keep rule rewritten 2026-11-05 as the classifier test, convention B). Replaces the drafts of PU 21 and PU 30. Draft, to append to the org-affiliation paragraph of the Source Descriptors definition:
   > This stripping applies to a specific named entity attached to the descriptor: an organization's proper name, or a specific named person who is identified elsewhere in the story. Capture only the bare descriptor ("organizer," "supporter," "friend," not "a friend of Jones"); the relationship to that entity typically belongs in Source Justification. Keep the words directly in front of the descriptor that sort it into a kind or subtype -- a noun ("affordable housing advocate," "tech billionaire," "PPP lender"), an identity word ("Black residents," "unhoused mother"), or an adjective that cannot be graded ("presidential candidate," "former felons," "full-time content creator"). Leave out words that describe or rate this particular source: words you could put "very" in front of, or say after "is" ("disgruntled," "vocal," "independent"), how long something has been true ("longtime," "lifelong"), and ranks or comparisons ("top," "second-largest," "leading"); these belong in Source Justification when they meet its definition. Also leave out anything that comes after the descriptor ("supporter of the settlements" → "supporter," "mother of four" → "mother," "collective of disgruntled neighbors" → "collective"), except a place for a person (below) and a group label whose head word means nothing on its own ("people who use drugs," "people close to the case"). Capture the words as one unbroken phrase from the article: if something you would leave out sits between them (such as an age, "transgender 18-year-old senior"), keep the identity word as its own descriptor ("transgender, senior") rather than joining the words. For a person or group of people, also keep a place only when the reporter attaches it to the source as part of the description ("South Shore resident," "resident of Chilobwe," "vendor from Juliaca"). Do not add a place that is only the story's setting or that you infer from elsewhere in the story, and do not turn "Name of Place" ("Melissa Estepa of Hayward") into a descriptor. For an organization, leave out where it is based ("Bay Area-based collective" → "collective"). When a place stands in front of a title (for example a state before an office, "Tolland County Republican Sheriff Dana Ruiz"), the place belongs to the title, not the Source Descriptor (Title "Tolland County Sheriff," Source Descriptors "Republican").

19. **Source Descriptors are story-level, not row-local** *[Folded into the consolidated v61 SD draft below, 2026-11-06.]* (Prompt Development 32 / `development-of-v59.md` item 62, adopted 2026-10-16; merged with Minor 9 / Prompt Updates 27). *Moved from Major 5, 2026-11-05: it changes the scope of an existing rule, not the schema.* Came from `GT-II/58-Louisiana_Shooting.csv` (Shamar Elkins): "the gunman" and "the father" appear only in a narrative sentence before his one sourced row, and v60's carry-forward only draws from the same source's earlier sourced statements. Draft, replacing the whole "Carrying source descriptors forward" paragraph of the system prompt (dropping its "use more cautiously than Title / when in doubt leave it out" caution):
   > Source Descriptors are a story-level value for each source. A word the reporter uses to characterize a source counts wherever it appears in the article body: in that source's own sourced statements, or in a sentence that is not itself a sourced statement. For example, "The police identified the gunman as Shamar Elkins, 31, and said he was the father of seven of the eight dead children" gives Shamar Elkins the Source Descriptors "gunman, father," even though his only sourced statement comes later. Apply a source's descriptors to every row for that source, including rows that come before the sentence where the descriptor appears, the same way Title of Source is carried forward and backfilled. When different parts of the story describe the same source differently, list the descriptors together, separated by commas (e.g., "protestor, artist"); a reporter revealing a second facet later does not mean the earlier one stopped being true. Replace a descriptor only when the source's status genuinely changes within the story (e.g., "candidate" becoming "councilmember" after an election result reported later). The same applies to every type of source: a Named Organization's category word (e.g., "nonprofit"), stated once, applies to all of that organization's rows.

   User prompt step 8: "Remember to apply carry-forward rule here outlined in the system_prompt's Source Descriptor section" becomes "Remember that Source Descriptors are a story-level value: apply the source's descriptors from anywhere in the article to every row for that source (system prompt, Source Descriptors)." The exclusion of a characterization inside another source's quote stays in Minor 4, not repeated here.

22. **Document vs. Named Organization: type by what the reporter credits** *(moved from Major 2 / Prompt Development 21, resolved 2026-11-06; `development-of-v59.md` PD 21 and PD 33).* Clarifies the existing Name of Source rule; no schema change. *Court sentence revised 2026-11-09 (user) after a corpus check of ~50 legal-document rows: legal documents follow the same credit test instead of always being Document; patched into the built v61 files.* Resolves PD 21's four shapes the same way (an organization's paper about its own product, a think tank's study, a news organization's analysis, a government agency's report): if the reporter names the organization, the organization is credited.
   **System prompt, Name of Source paragraph (line 91):** replace the sentence from "If a named organization formally releases or stands behind..." through "...Otherwise leave the Name of Source as null." with:
   > When a statement comes from a document, decide the Type of Source by what the reporter credits at that point in the text. If the reporter names the organization the document comes from -- whether the organization produced, published or commissioned it, or keeps it as a public record ("a Halvorsen Institute study," "a Tribune analysis," "data from the county health department," "Riverside Fire Department reports") -- the Type of Source is Named Organization and the Name of Source is that organization's name. Otherwise the Type of Source is Document: when no organization is named as the document's origin at that point ("a recent study," "court records show"), when the organization is named only as the document's subject or target ("a lawsuit against the county"), when only the document's own title or an index name is given ("the Ridgeline Prosperity Index"), or when the material is internal or leaked (Note 14). Court and legal documents -- petitions, briefs, motions, complaints, lawsuits, testimony, depositions, affidavits, exhibits and other court records -- follow the same test. When the reporter credits the document ("the lawsuit claims," "court records show," "according to the brief"), the Type of Source is Document, even if the court where it was filed or the party that filed it is mentioned ("a brief filed in the Ninth Circuit," "the lawsuit, filed by the Harbor Tenants Union, claims"). When the reporter credits a named person or organization as speaking through the document ("the union argued in its lawsuit," "Tomas Byrne alleges in the suit," "Byrne said in a deposition"), that person or organization is the source. A court or judge is the source for its own ruling, opinion or order: "the Ninth Circuit ruled" is Named Organization; "Judge Lena Ortiz wrote in the ruling" is Named Person. For a Document, the Name of Source is the document's own title (see Note 14) only if the reporter includes it; otherwise it is null. For a Named Organization, the document's genre word ("study," "data") is not its Source Descriptor.

   **User prompt, step 5:** replace the sentence "Remember: if a named organization formally releases or stands behind the document in its own name (e.g., a press release, white paper, or position note or memo), reclassify the Type of Source as Named Organization instead, and use that organization's name as the Name of Source -- do not supply a publisher's name the reporter has not actually attributed the document to (system_prompt Note 14)." with:

If no organization is named as the document's origin, or it is named only as the document's subject (e.g., "a lawsuit against the county"), the Type of Source is Document. For court and legal documents, use the same test: Document when the reporter credits the document ("the lawsuit claims," "court records show"), even if the filer is mentioned; the named person or organization when the reporter credits them as speaking through it ("the union argued in its lawsuit," "Byrne testified"); and the court or judge for its own ruling, opinion or order.
